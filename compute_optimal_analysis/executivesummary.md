# Spectral-Chinchilla executive summary

Spectral-Chinchilla is a research framework for testing whether compute allocation leaves reproducible signatures in transformer weight and activation spectra. It combines a configurable decoder-only language model, a Chinchilla/IsoFLOP allocator, activation-covariance capture, a torch-free NumPy/SciPy random-matrix engine, and reversible singular-value lesion experiments.

The central comparison is a three-regime family at fixed compute, with undertrained, compute-optimal, and overtrained allocations. For each analyzable attention or MLP matrix the framework measures Marchenko-Pastur bulk agreement, both spectral edges, selectable tail estimates, stable rank and entropy, unfolded level statistics, Brody interpolation, number variance, and alignment between right singular vectors and activation-covariance eigenvectors. Lesion experiments independently remove top, bulk, or bottom singular tranches and measure validation-loss and perplexity changes; a separate descending-decile mode reproduces the supplied dual-end archive.

Phase II makes scientific choices explicit. MP scale may come from analytic quantile matching, a hard-edge-safe integrated-ECDF descendant of the historical KDE fit, FARMS-pooled fixed-ratio submatrices, or a Lanczos–Cholesky one-cut support estimate. Unfolding may use Chebyshev, monotone spline, adaptive Gaussian-CDF, or raw ranks. Tail estimation may use CSN cutoff selection, Hill, fixed-cutoff MLE, or rank regression. Activation alignment may use squared pairwise projections, principal angles, or normalized projector trace.

The two 2025 methods are implemented according to the supplied code as well as the papers rather than the shorthand in the task. FARMS is Fixed-Aspect-Ratio Matrix Subsampling: multiple equal-shape windows produce an averaged pooled ESD, with the released ratio defined as sampled columns divided by sampled rows. It is not a pointwise normalization map. The Lanczos method uses normalized Gaussian probes, double full reorthogonalization, adaptive recurrence diagnostics, a reference modified-tail Cholesky construction, support `(alpha-beta)^2` to `(alpha+beta)^2`, probe-averaged VEST evaluation, and finite-Jacobi Ritz spike counting. Constant-tail finite-section poles remain an explicit alternative. Its consistency is conditional on a positive one-cut bulk with square-root edges and separated right spikes.

Phase III turns the framework into a fail-closed offline HPC package. The v2 repair makes target-token budgets authoritative across recycled partial batches and skipped updates; records attempted targets, forwarded positions, and explicitly labeled `6ND` proxies; rejects collapsed requested N/D interventions unless explicitly allowed as calibration; and requires fresh output roots. A connected-host staging utility caches the exact dataset/tokenizer assets and emits checksums; compute jobs set all offline environment switches and load only local token arrays. The training layer supports BF16/FP16 autocast, TF32, optional model compilation, pinned asynchronous transfers, accelerator-resident float32 covariance accumulation, and reduced accelerator SVD. Only final covariance matrices or reduced SVD factors are converted to NumPy for the pure engine. Weight/lesion SVD precision is independently selected with `--analysis-dtype` (float64 by default), and fit/detector/plot outputs carry a consistent spectrum domain plus explicit availability diagnostics. The SLURM harness exposes three historical reproduction tracks and a fourth Golden synthesis using FARMS, Lanczos, monotone-spline unfolding, CSN tails, dual-end overlap, and spectral lesions.

The scientific claims are deliberately separated into two categories. Synthetic Wishart, GOE, Poisson, and Pareto checks are calibration tests with known answers. Claims about trained transformers, including heavy-tailed deep layers, compute-optimal spectral collapse, and enhanced information at the smallest singular values, are empirical hypotheses and are never treated as unit-test invariants.

The current hardening pass also makes bounded-tail likelihoods mathematically conditional on `xmax`, qualifies rank-deficient activation overlap, contains detector-only Lanczos failures, recovers normal FP16 scaler overflow, validates collapse only within selected cells, and atomically claims every output root. The `rmt/` package imports only the standard library, NumPy, and SciPy. The training framework is confined to `models/` and `pipelines/`. Every lesion is reversible, every random operation accepts a seed, and experiment outputs record requested and realized parameter/token allocations together with the complete spectral-method configuration.
